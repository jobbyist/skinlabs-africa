Razor bumps are not a personality. They are hair forced through inflamed skin, then sun asked to paint the mark brown and keep it. On South African jaws, necks and bikini lines the cycle is mechanical first and cosmetic second.

## Quick answer

Pseudofolliculitis barbae is curved hair re-entering the follicle or piercing the skin beside it. Close shaves, dry shaves and stretching the skin make it worse. Heat, sweat and friction from collars and PPE keep it going. Post-inflammatory pigment then sits in the sun and looks like dirt that will not wash off. Change the tool and the angle before you buy another oil.

## The mechanics, not the myth

Hair that curls as it grows — common across African and mixed textures — does not exit the follicle in a polite straight line. A razor that cuts below the surface leaves a sharp tip under the skin. That tip punches into the wall. The bump is inflammation. The dark ring is melanin responding to injury.

This is not poor hygiene. Scrubbing the bump with a net sponge or a menthol aftershave is how you add PIH to the original insult. The net sponge briefing on this desk already covered bodies. Faces and necks collect the same mistake in miniature.

Beard culture and school or workplace rules collide here. A clean-shaven policy plus a coarse curl pattern is a dermatology clinic waiting to happen. Some people can shave daily with a guard and stay quiet. Some cannot. The difference is anatomy, not discipline.

## Why South African climate joins the fight

Sweat under a security shirt, a kitchen, a mine lamp strap, a school blazer collar. Occlusion plus salt plus heat is folliculitis weather. KZN humidity adds yeast pressure on chests and backs; the jawline still follows the blade.

Highveld dry air in winter cracks the skin around the follicle. You then shave a rough surface. Spring wind puts dust in the same nicks. Summer UV locks the pigment.

Isaacs and colleagues’ photoprotection advice for South African dermatoses applies after the bump exists. A healing follicle under noon sun is a pigment factory. SPF on the jaw is not vanity. It is how you stop a grey-brown map.

## Tools that change the physics

A single-blade or a quality safety razor used with the grain, on hair that has been softened with water, produces fewer subterranean tips than a five-blade cartridge yanked against the grain for extra close.

Electric foil shavers and precision trimmers that leave a millimetre of hair often beat a baby-smooth shave. The millimetre is the point. Hair that has a chance to exit the follicle does not have to punch sideways.

Depilatory creams burn some skins and spare others. Patch test on the arm, not on the neck. Ingrowns still happen if you scrape the paste off with a sharp edge.

Waxing and threading on coarse curl can yank the follicle and still leave a bump. They are not automatically gentler. Laser and electrolysis belong in rooms that treat skin of colour every week, with settings that do not assume a pale trial photo. Burns on the jaw become PIH that lasts a year.

## The neck is a different organ

The neck has thinner skin, more friction from collars and more chance of stretching during a shave. Most scars from PFB live under the jaw. Shave last there. Use a guard. Stop when it weeps.

Aftershave alcohol is perfume plus solvent. It feels like medicine because it stings. Sting is not proof of cleanliness. A bland water-based lotion or a prescribed topical is the after step.

## Women, bikini lines and the same physics

The briefing title says blade. The body does not care about gender. Bikini, underarm and nape hair that curls will ingrow after a close shave the week before a holiday. Stretching the skin on a sunlounger after a wax is how people collect both bumps and pigment.

Do not put a glycolic wipe on a fresh wax. Do not put an illegal lightening cream on a bikini line because the marks look uneven. Azelaic acid and niacinamide are the brighter, slower tools this desk already trusts under SA sun. They still need the skin to be closed.

## Acne versus PFB versus keloid

Acne sits in a comedone pattern and often involves the T-zone. PFB follows the shave pattern. Keloids after PFB are more common in some families and on the beard area in men with African ancestry. A bump that keeps growing after the hair is gone is not a bump you pick.

Picking is how you convert a two-week papule into a five-year scar. Leave it. See a dermatologist if the map is spreading or if you have thick scars already.

Folliculitis from bacteria or from Malassezia can mimic PFB on the chest. Humid Durban gyms produce the yeast version. If the bumps are identical and itchy on the back, read the yeast briefing. If they sit only where the razor went, stay here.

## What to put on it

When the skin is intact: a mild leave-on with azelaic acid or a low-strength retinoid mapped by a clinician can reduce both inflammation and the pigment. Hydrocortisone for three days on a fierce flare is sometimes used under advice. It is not a lifestyle.

Benzoyl peroxide can help bacterial load and can bleach towels and collars. Niacinamide is the easy companion. Fragrance-free. No menthol. No “ice” splash.

SPF every morning on the beard area if you shave. Tinted if pigment is the complaint. Hats still help the neck.

Prescription topical antibiotics and, in selected cases, oral medicines exist. So does professional extraction that you should not copy in a bathroom mirror with a pin.

## Work rules and dignity

A security firm that demands a bone-smooth jaw on a man whose follicles punch back is choosing a medical outcome. Reasonable accommodation is a trimmer guard, not a disciplinary hearing. The same is true of school codes that punish texture.

This is not a labour-law treatise. It is a note that the blade policy is a skin policy.

## Gyms, steam and after sport

Steam opens the field and then you shave. That sequence feels efficient and produces more nicks. Shave after a short warm shower, not after twenty minutes of steam and a towel rub that already inflamed the follicle.

Sweat in a beard that is growing out will itch. Wash. Do not dry-shave the itch at 22:00.

## Growing it out

The ugliest fortnight is the grow-out. Hair that was trapped starts to exit. Resist the razor for that window if your goal is a reset. A clean trimmer line can keep you employable while the map calms.

Oil does not dissolve a trapped hair. A sterile needle in untrained hands becomes a scar. If you can see a loop, a professional can lift it. If you cannot, leave it.

## Colourism and the “clean” jaw

A smooth, pale-looking jaw has been sold as professional. Pigment on the follicle is then treated as dirt. It is melanin. Treat the inflammation. Do not bleach the beard zone with mercury. South Africa banned those cosmetics decades ago and they still appear. This desk has written that story. It applies here.

## A protocol for the next ten days

Day 1 to 3: stop the close shave. Trimmer only. Bland cleanser. Azelaic or niacinamide if you already tolerate them. SPF on the jaw.

Day 4 to 7: same. No new acid. No scrub.

Day 8 to 10: if you must blade, once, with the grain, on softened hair, single pass, no stretching. Stop at weep.

Photograph in the same bathroom light. If the map is keloid or rapidly spreading, skip the rest of this list and book a dermatologist.

## Clinic tools

Intralesional steroid for a thick scar. Laser hair reduction in skilled hands. Topical retinoid programmes. Antibiotics when infection is real. None of these are a Saturday special at a mall kiosk that also does brows.

Ask how often they treat Fitzpatrick V and VI beards. If the answer is vague, leave.

## Children and first shaves

A teenager’s first razor plus a school inspection is how PFB starts early. Teach the guard first. Teach that blood is a stop sign, not a badge. Do not hand over a five-blade gift set as a rite of passage without the sentence about grain.

## FAQ

Can I shave every day? Some people can with a guard. Daily bone-smooth against the grain is the usual trigger.

Does aftershave heal bumps? Alcohol aftershave does not.

Will laser make it worse? Poorly set laser on dark skin can burn and pigment. Well-set laser in the right room can reduce hair and therefore bumps.

Is coconut oil a treatment? It is an occlusive some people like and some break out on. It does not change the angle of the hair.

## Money on South African shelves

Pharmacy trimmers cost less over a year than a monthly tube of miracle bump oil. A simple azelaic from a labelled brand beats an unlabelled “bump stopper” with a celebrity sticker.

Replace blades before they tug. A blunt blade is a plough.

## Heat, cars and the after-shave commute

A freshly shaved neck in a hot taxi against a polyester collar is a friction experiment. Loose cotton for the commute if you can. Do not add a scarf that rubs.

## When it is not PFB

Tinea on the beard, hidradenitis under the jaw, acne keloidalis nuchae on the nape — these are different maps. Acne keloidalis nuchae is a nape disease that needs a dermatologist early, not a closer fade. If the bumps live on the back of the head after a tight cut, stop the close fade and get a name for it.

## Editorial close

The blade is a tool. The enemy is a sharp tip under curved hair, plus heat, plus sun on the mark. Change the length, the pass and the aftercare. Keep the SPF on the jaw. Leave the stall bleach on the stall.

A close shave is a style. It is not a medical requirement. Treat it like a procedure when your follicles have already told you they disagree.

## Aftercare the night you ignore

Night is when people pick. Put a bland cream on and leave the room. A phone torch and a mirror is a procedure room you are not qualified to run. If a loop of hair is clearly out, a clean tweezer can lift the loop — not the root, the loop. If you cannot see the loop, your fingers have no job.

Bedding that sheds fibres into a freshly shaved neck will itch at 02:00. Change the pillowcase more often during a flare. That is not wellness. That is fewer fibres in a hole.

## Military, police and corporate uniforms

Starch in a collar is cardboard on inflamed follicles. Soften the first wash. A clean vest under a hard shirt reduces rub. Helmet straps on the jaw need the same logic as PPE maskne: dry, clean fabric, not a tighter strap.

If a medical note is the only way a workplace will accept a guard length, get the note. This is cheaper than a keloid.

## Barbers and fades

A skin fade that crosses the beard line is a close shave by another name. Tell the barber you bump. A good barber already knows. A rushed chair will still go to the bone because the picture on the wall is bone. You can leave.

Clipper burn on the nape plus a tight collar is how acne keloidalis starts for some men. Stop the extra-close nape if bumps live there. Ask for a slightly longer landing.

## Partners, perfume and shared bathrooms

His aftershave is not your toner. Shared blades rust and share blood. One person, one head, replaced on a calendar. Perfume on a nick is a chemical burn with a brand name.

## Travel and humid weeks

A Durban wedding after a Joburg shave is occlusion plus humidity plus a collar. Grow a millimetre before you travel. Pack the trimmer. Hotel razors are stamped metal and optimism.

## What the evidence stack is

Dermatology texts describe PFB as a foreign-body inflammatory reaction to hair. Skin-of-colour reviews list it among the most common beard complaints in men with tightly coiled hair. Treatment hierarchies start with hair-length change, then anti-inflammatories, then medical hair reduction. Cosmetics sit in the middle as comfort, not cure. Photoprotection sits across the whole map because PIH is the long shadow.

That is enough science for a bathroom shelf. You do not need a patented peptide to accept that a millimetre of hair is a medical device.

## Relapse is the default

One clean month does not retire the anatomy. A holiday close shave will reopen the file. Keep the guard in the bag the way you keep SPF in the bag. The briefing is a habit, not a course you pass.
