A pimple is supposed to flatten. A cut is supposed to pale. On some chests, jaws and earlobes in this country the tissue keeps building after the injury has closed. That is not a hygiene failure and it is not vanity. It is how some skin of colour lays down collagen when the signal to stop arrives late.

## Quick answer

Keloids grow beyond the original wound. Hypertrophic scars stay inside it but stand proud. Both are more common on darker phototypes, on the chest, shoulders, jaw, earlobes and sites of acne or piercing. First-line clinic care is usually intralesional corticosteroid, sometimes combined with other agents. Home scrubs, lemon and unlabelled scar gels do not turn off fibroblasts. Sun on a new scar is how the mound also becomes a dark plaque.

## One-sentence answer

Treat raised scars on South African skin as a medical pattern: protect them from UV, stop picking, and see someone who injects scars on melanin-rich skin every week, not a stall that sells bleaching cream.
