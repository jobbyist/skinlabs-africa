Your darker skin tone still needs sunscreen every day in South Africa. The gap is not whether you burn. The gap is whether the formula you can actually wear also blocks UVA and visible light, the two wavelengths that keep feeding melasma and post-inflammatory marks.

## Quick answer

South African UV sits in the high-to-extreme range for large parts of the year, including winter on the Highveld. Melanin-rich skin burns less often, so people skip SPF and then watch pigmentation stall. Tinted mineral sunscreens are the practical fix: they cut visible light as well as UV, they reduce the grey cast, and they are the format local dermatologists keep pointing to after the 2024 integrated sun-protection review for this population.

## What the South African evidence actually says

A 2024 review in the International Journal of Dermatology, led by South African clinicians including Prof Ncoza Dlova, set out integrated sun-protection advice for this country rather than recycling northern-hemisphere guidance. The Stellenbosch Dermatology Institute summarised the same paper for patients: daily protection is not seasonal here. The climate is subtropical. UV stays relatively high year-round. Groups that need extra caution include children, very fair skin, people with albinism, immunosuppressed patients and outdoor workers. Darker skin is less likely to produce the classic sunburn-to-skin-cancer story that dominates European campaigns, but it is more likely to produce hyperpigmentation and accelerated photoageing when UV and visible light keep landing on the face.

That last sentence is the one most marketing skips. Skin cancer risk is not zero in deeper skin tones, and late diagnosis is a real problem because lesions look different. But the day-to-day reason you should wear SPF in Johannesburg in July is pigmentation, not a red chest after the beach.

The same review notes that melasma is more common in skin of colour and is triggered by UVB, UVA and visible light. Tinted sunscreens reduce visible-light transmission by a reported 93 to 98 percent in the literature cited there. Untinted chemical SPF can be excellent against UVB and still leave the visible-light door open. If your dark patches keep returning despite a diligent SPF habit, that door is often the missing piece.

## Why a white cast is a health problem, not a vanity problem

Here is the thing. A grey film on melanin-rich skin is the reason people under-apply, skip reapplication, or abandon sunscreen entirely. South Africa's UV index regularly hits the extreme category of 11 and above in summer. If the product you bought was optimised on lighter panels, the invisible finish was never tested on you.

Under-application is how a labelled SPF 50 behaves like a much lower number. Dermatologists here still use the two-finger rule from the local review: a line of product along the index and middle fingers, from palmar crease to fingertip, for each body region, 15 to 30 minutes before you go out, then again every two to four hours depending on sweat and activity. That is a lot of product. If it looks ashy, you will not do it.

Tinted formulas solve two jobs at once. Iron oxides and pigment blends absorb and scatter visible light. They also camouflage the chalkiness of zinc and titanium so the film looks like skin instead of plaster. You do not need a full-coverage foundation. You need enough tint that the mineral load disappears on your undertone.

## UVA, UVB, visible light and the Highveld

UVB is the sunburn wavelength. UVA is the ageing and pigment wavelength that passes through cloud and window glass. Visible light, especially the high-energy blue-violet band, is now treated as a pigment trigger in medium and deep skin, not a cosmetic afterthought.

Johannesburg sits at about 1,750 metres. Altitude increases UV intensity. Winter Highveld days are bright, dry and deceptively cool. People dress for the cold and forget the face is still in full sun on the N1. Durban and the Garden Route add humidity and reflected light off water. Cape Town adds long, clear summer days and a culture of outdoor living. One country, several UV personalities, same rule: daily face SPF if you care about marks.

Indoor visible light from screens is sometimes mentioned as a pigment contributor. The evidence is weaker than for sunlight. Do not buy a blue-light serum before you have a tinted SPF habit. Priorities matter.

## How to choose a tinted SPF that will actually get used

Look at the label the way a dermatologist would, not the way a shelf talker would.

- Broad spectrum with a stated UVA claim, not just an SPF number.
- SPF 30 as a floor for daily urban wear, SPF 50 or 50+ when you are outside for a stretch. Local clinic advice for this climate leans to SPF 50+ when exposure is real.
- A tint that matches your undertone closely enough that you do not need a second base layer if you do not want one.
- A texture you will reapply. A gorgeous laboratory finish that pills under your moisturiser is a failed product for you.
- Availability at Clicks, Dis-Chem, Dermastore or a clinic you already visit. A formula you can only import once a year will not be on your bathroom shelf in November.

South African clinic brands and international pharmacy lines both sell tinted options now. Compare the iron oxide story and the feel on your jawline, not the Instagram swatch on someone else's lighting.

## What this means layered with pigmentation actives

Sunscreen does not fade existing melanin. It stops the signal that keeps making more. That is why azelaic acid, niacinamide, tranexamic acid, retinoids and prescription hydroquinone all look weak if you skip SPF. You are treating and triggering on the same day.

If you are using a dermatologist-supervised hydroquinone cycle, daily tinted SPF is not optional. If you are using over-the-counter brightening serums from a pharmacy aisle, the same rule applies, just with slower expectations.

Reapply over makeup with a tinted stick, compact or spray marketed for that job. Perfection is not the goal. Coverage of the high points of the face, again, after lunch, is.

## Who should pay attention

Anyone managing melasma, PIH after acne or razor bumps, or uneven tone on Fitzpatrick IV to VI skin. Outdoor workers. Drivers who sit in UVA through a windscreen. Parents packing school bags who still skip their own face. People who tried mineral SPF once, hated the cast, and decided sunscreen was not for dark skin. That last group is the one this briefing is written for.

## Ingredients and product types worth knowing

Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the mineral filters. Iron oxides supply the visible-light block and the tint. Chemical filters such as Tinosorb grades, Uvinul A Plus and well-studied UVB filters can be excellent in hybrid formulas. Hybrid tinted products are often the easiest to wear in heat because they avoid a heavy paste.

Avoid treating reef-safe or clean as a protection claim. Those words are marketing categories. The job is UV plus visible light, on your face, in this climate.

## South African context

Retail reality matters. You can buy a usable tinted SPF at a major pharmacy chain without a clinic appointment. You can also overpay for a boutique bottle that is no better on UVA. Check the PA or UVA circle, the tint range, and whether the product melts in a hot car. Summer storage in a bathroom that hits high twenties will shorten the life of some chemical filters. Replace yearly if the texture splits or the smell turns.

Medical aid rarely covers cosmetic sunscreen. Budget for it the way you budget for toothpaste. A mid-range pharmacy tinted SPF used every morning beats a prestige bottle used twice a week.

If you sweat through security queues, school runs and load-shedding walks between buildings, keep a mini tinted stick in the bag. Reapplication is the unglamorous half of the protocol. A single morning layer is not a force field.

People with melasma should also think about heat, not only light. Gym classes, saunas and sitting in a parked car can flare pigment even when UV is blocked. Cool the face after heat exposure. Do not stack aggressive acids the same morning you have a long outdoor event.

Hats still earn their place. A wide brim cuts the dose that reaches the forehead and temples, which is where many South African patients first notice a butterfly pattern. Sunglasses protect the thin skin around the eyes, an area that stings when sunscreen migrates.

Children need their own conversation. Paediatric skin and very fair phototypes follow stricter shade-and-clothing rules in the local review. This briefing is aimed at adults managing pigment. Do not improvise adult tinted makeup SPF as a baby product.

Finally, check moles and odd patches. Pigment disorders and skin cancers can coexist. A mark that changes, bleeds or looks unlike your usual PIH needs a clinician, not another serum. Late presentation in deeper skin tones is well described internationally. Early photographs in daylight help a teledermatology consult if you cannot reach a metro clinic quickly.

## What to do this week

1. Put a tinted SPF next to your moisturiser tonight so the morning decision is already made.
2. Swatch it on your jaw in daylight, not bathroom yellow light.
3. Use the two-finger quantity on your face and neck tomorrow, even if you are only going to the office.
4. Set a phone reminder to reapply after lunch if you walk outside.
5. If pigmentation is your main complaint, book a dermatologist who treats skin of colour rather than adding a fourth brightening serum.

## FAQ

Does darker skin need SPF in winter? Yes. UVA and visible light do not take July off on the Highveld.

Is chemical SPF enough? It can be enough for UV. It is often not enough for visible-light-driven pigment. Tint closes that gap.

Can I use my foundation as sunscreen? Only if it is a dedicated SPF product applied at sunscreen quantity. Most foundations are used too thinly.

## Building a realistic weekday protocol

Monday to Friday is where most South African SPF habits die. You leave home in the half-dark, sit in traffic, walk a hot pavement between the car and the office, then sit near a window that does nothing to block UVA. A weekday protocol is therefore not the same as a beach protocol. Keep a bottle at the desk. Reapply before the lunch walk. If security will not let you carry a large tube, a stick in a pocket still covers cheeks, nose and forehead, which is where pigment concentrates.

Weekend sport changes the math again. Sweat thins the film. Reapply after the first set, not after the match. Hats on junior cricket sidelines protect adults as well as children. Braai smoke is not UV, but the hours standing in a garden in November absolutely are.

## What broad spectrum should mean on a local label

An SPF number mainly reflects UVB. Without a UVA claim you have half a product for pigment. Look for UVA-PF, PA ratings, or the UVA circle. SAHPRA and standards conversations around claims have been tightening over time; treat vague all-day protection language with suspicion. Water resistance is a timed laboratory claim, not a swimming holiday guarantee.

If two bottles cost the same and only one publishes a UVA figure, that is the bottle. If neither does, the tinted mineral option is the safer guess for visible light.

## Edge cases

Albinism and very fair phototypes need clothing and shade first, sunscreen second, and medical follow-up for lesions. This briefing does not replace that care pathway. Immunosuppressed patients, including some living with HIV on photosensitising medication, also sit in a higher-risk group flagged in local advice. Ask the prescribing clinician about extra sun care rather than crowdsourcing a forum.

People who wear hijab or other covering still need SPF on the face, hands and any exposed neck. Covered skin can still receive bounce from pavement and water. The habit is the exposed skin, every time.

Athletes at altitude — Magaliesberg weekends, Drakensberg trails — pick up dose fast. Pack more product than seems polite. Lips need an SPF balm because they pigment and burn without drama until they peel.

If you have tried five tinted SPFs and all oxidise dark on your jaw, you may be reacting to iron oxides or to a poorly matched undertone. Return the bottle. Swatch in daylight. A mismatched tint that you never wear is not protection.

Your face is not a European clinical panel. Choose the film you will repeat.
