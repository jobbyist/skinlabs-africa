Azelaic acid is one of the few brightening ingredients that earns its reputation on melanin-rich skin without the legal and safety mess of unregulated hydroquinone. For South African faces dealing with melasma and post-inflammatory marks under extreme UV, that combination of evidence and tolerability matters more than a ten-day miracle claim.

## Quick answer

Azelaic acid at cosmetic strengths around 10 percent, and at prescription 15 to 20 percent, can fade pigment and calm acne-related marks. A classic comparison found 20 percent azelaic acid performed in the same conversation as 4 percent hydroquinone for melasma over months, not days. Pair it with daily tinted SPF or you will treat and retrigger in the same week. It is slower than illegal lightening creams. That slowness is the point.

## Why pigment behaves differently here

Melasma is driven by UVB, UVA, visible light, heat and hormones. South Africa supplies the first four in abundance and the fifth whenever pregnancy, contraception or thyroid change enters the picture. Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation follows acne, eczema, insect bites and razor bumps more aggressively as constitutive melanin increases.

A review on facial hyperpigmentation in skin of colour summarises topical options that have actually been studied in deeper phototypes. Azelaic acid sits there with niacinamide, retinoids, tranexamic acid and carefully supervised hydroquinone. It is not a folk remedy. It is a dicarboxylic acid with anti-inflammatory and anti-tyrosinase activity and a useful effect on abnormal melanocytes while leaving typical ones relatively less disturbed.

That selectivity is why it is often better tolerated than a blunt bleach.

## How to use it without wrecking the barrier

Start every other night if you are new to acids. A thin layer on clean, dry skin. Moisturiser after. SPF in the morning without debate. Stinging for a few minutes can happen. Persistent burning means you dropped the barrier, usually because you also used a BHA, a retinoid and a vitamin C that week.

You can combine azelaic acid with niacinamide. Many people do. Combining it with a retinoid is possible if a clinician maps the nights. Combining it with unsupervised high-dose hydroquinone from a market stall is how people end up with exogenous ochronosis. South Africa banned high hydroquinone in cosmetics in 1990 for reasons that have not expired.

Give a proper trial twelve weeks. Photographs in the same window light help. If nothing has shifted at all, you may have dermal pigment, ongoing heat exposure, or a diagnosis that is not PIH.

## Melasma versus PIH versus a mark you should get checked

Melasma is usually symmetric, blotchy, worse on the cheeks, forehead or upper lip, and flares with sun and heat. PIH sits where the pimple or scratch was. A single changing plaque is neither. Skin cancers and other disorders can be missed on dark skin because teaching images were pale for decades. When in doubt, a dermatologist.

Heat deserves its own paragraph. A car parked in Durban, a hot yoga room, a kitchen shift. Melasma cares. Cool water on the face after heat is a cheap intervention. Chasing a new serum while sitting in a sun-soaked taxi queue is not a protocol.

## Who should pay attention

Anyone with stubborn marks after acne. Pregnant people who cannot use certain retinoids or hydroquinone and need a clinician to confirm what is appropriate. Men with beard bumps and grey-brown residue on the jaw. People who tried vitamin C alone and saw nothing because they never blocked visible light.

## Ingredients that sit well next to it

Niacinamide at 5 to 10 percent. Tranexamic acid in leave-on products. Azelaic’s cousin-in-strategy, alpha arbutin, at cosmetic doses. Retinoids for turnover once the barrier is calm. Tinted mineral SPF as the non-negotiable base.

Vitamin C can help but L-ascorbic acid is unstable in heat and can sting a compromised winter barrier. Derivatives are easier for many South African bathrooms.

## South African shelves

Pharmacy aisles now carry azelaic acid serums and creams from local and international brands. Prescription strengths live with dermatologists. Price varies more than mechanism. A well-formulated 10 percent used daily beats a 20 percent used twice then abandoned.

Watch the rest of the formula. Fragrance and high alcohol can undo the gentleness that made you choose azelaic acid. If you buy online, use a retailer that stores product out of direct sun.

SAHPRA’s job is medicines and the border of cosmetics. Azelaic acid at cosmetic levels is widely sold. That does not make every bottle equal. Look for a real ingredient list and a manufacturer who can be named.

## What to do this week

1. Start or restart azelaic acid on alternate nights only.
2. Keep niacinamide if you already tolerate it.
3. Wear tinted SPF every morning, including desk days.
4. Take a baseline photo.
5. Book a dermatologist if marks are deep, expanding, or you are tempted by an unlabelled lightening cream.

## FAQ

Can I use it in the morning? Some people do, under SPF. Night is simpler if you sting.

Is it safe in pregnancy? Clinicians often consider it when other actives are off the table, but that is an individual medical decision, not a blog permission slip.

Will it bleach my whole face? Used as directed it targets blotchy pigment more than your baseline tone. That is a feature.

Expectancy management keeps people on the protocol. Illegal mercury creams lighten fast because they are toxic. Azelaic acid asks for consistency through a South African summer, which is a long ask. Build the habit around the SPF you already committed to. Put the azelaic tube next to the toothbrush.

If you also treat acne, azelaic acid is a convenient overlap. It can reduce inflammatory lesions and the marks they leave. It will not replace isotretinoin or a full cystic-acne plan. Severe acne still needs a doctor.

Peels using salicylic acid appear in skin-of-colour literature for melasma when done by people who understand PIH risk. Home peeling after watching a video is how PIH gets worse. Leave the high percentages in clinic.

Diet threads will tell you to cut dairy or add glutathione tablets. Evidence for oral glutathione as a pigment fixer is not the foundation of this briefing. Eat reasonably. Sleep. Wear the tinted SPF. The unsexy stack wins.

When a mark finally fades, maintenance begins. Melasma is chronic. Dropping all actives and holidaying without a hat is how November undoes July. Keep a simple trio: cleanser that does not strip, azelaic or niacinamide, tinted SPF. Add retinoid nights when life is calm.

If you work outdoors — farm, construction, sports coaching, traffic — no serum outruns dose. Clothing and shade do more than an extra dropper. Use the serum anyway. Just do not pretend it is a parasol.

Your skin did not get the memo that a brightening launch would outrun the UV index. Chemistry plus climate plus consistency is the whole story.

## Building a 12-week grid

Week 1 to 2: azelaic three nights, moisturiser, SPF. Week 3 to 6: azelaic most nights if comfortable. Week 7 to 12: add a retinoid two nights that are not your most irritated nights, or add tranexamic if pigment is clearly hormonal and a clinician agrees. Photograph day 1, day 42, day 84. No other changes if you want to know what worked.

Holiday weeks do not pause UV. They pause discipline. Pack the same three products. Leave the new boutique vial at home.

## Colourism sits next to this conversation

People chase fast lightening for reasons that are social, not dermatologic. This desk has written about illegal hydroquinone and mercury already. Azelaic acid is not a political solution to colourism. It is a safer tool if uneven tone is genuinely your goal. You do not owe anyone a lighter face. You do owe yourself a product that will not wreck your kidneys.

## Clinic procedures

Chemical peels, tranexamic injections and lasers exist in metro rooms. They can help selected patients and they can worsen PIH when settings ignore skin of colour. Ask how often the practitioner treats Fitzpatrick V and VI. Ask about blistering rates. If the answer is vague, leave.

## What improvement looks like

Edges of a patch blur. Makeup sits more evenly. Contrast between mark and baseline drops. Complete erasure is uncommon for deep melasma. Anyone promising a blank slate in a week is selling a different chemistry, usually a worse one.

Stay with the boring stack. It is the one that survives a South African year.

## Layering order that usually behaves

Cleanse. Wait a minute. Azelaic acid. Wait. Moisturiser. Morning is cleanse, moisturiser if needed, tinted SPF. Keep vitamin C to mornings only if you use it, and not on a raw barrier.

If you use a prescription topical steroid on the face, that is a short, supervised tool. It is not a brightening plan. Steroid abuse in lightening mixes is a separate emergency this desk has already flagged.

## Travel and heat maps

A January trip to KZN is a pigment stress test. Pack more SPF than azelaic acid. Reapply. Sit in shade for the middle hours when you can. The 10:00 to 16:00 window in local sun advice exists for a reason.

You can enjoy the sea. You cannot out-serum it.

## Men, beards and grey-brown rings

Folliculitis on the beard leaves rings of PIH that look like dirt. They are pigment. Azelaic acid can help both the inflammation and the mark. Change blades. Do not dry-shave. Do not put a glycolic wipe on micro-cuts.

## Makeup as camouflage while you wait

A mid-coverage tinted SPF is camouflage and treatment in one. Heavy concealer without SPF is a mask that still lets UVA through. If you love makeup, layer it on top of the sunscreen film, not instead of it.

Twelve weeks is long. Camouflage is how people stay sane enough to finish the protocol.

## Why 20 percent and 10 percent feel different

Prescription 20 percent creams are often more gritty and more effective per classic trials. Cosmetic 10 percent serums are easier to buy and easier to apply under makeup. Start with what you will use. Escalate if a clinician thinks you have room.

Some formulas use azelaic acid in a derivative or a lower percentage plus soothing agents. That can be a good on-ramp. It can also be a way to sell water. Read the percentage. If it is missing, treat the claim as soft.

## PIH after procedures

Fillers, extractions and laser hair removal can leave marks on deeper skin. Azelaic acid after the skin has closed can be part of aftercare. Starting it on an open extraction is how you pick up more pigment. Timing belongs to the person who did the procedure.

## Neighbourhood pharmacies and online grey markets

A sealed box from Clicks is a different object from a loose tube in a marketplace listing with no batch code. Counterfeit brightening products ride the same demand as illegal hydroquinone. If the discount looks unreal, the contents might be too.

Keep receipts. If a product inflames within days, you have a path back to the retailer.

## A last pass on honesty

Azelaic acid will not give you a different ancestry. It may give you less contrast between a mark and the skin you already have. That is a good medical goal. It is also enough of a goal. Anything else belongs to culture, not to this bottle.

## Weekend checklist

Saturday morning: SPF before errands. Saturday night: azelaic if the face is calm. Sunday: photos if it is a checkpoint week. If you attended a day event without a hat, do not "catch up" with extra acid. Catch up with rest and more cream.

That is two thousand words of saying the same true thing from different doors. Use the door you will actually walk through.
